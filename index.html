The lion ( lat. Panthera leo ) is a species of carnivorous mammal , one of five representatives of the genus panthera ( Panthera ), belonging to the subfamily of big cats within the family Felidae . Along with the tiger , it is the largest of the currently living cats; the weight of some males can reach 250 kg . It is difficult to say reliably which is more massive: the largest subspecies of lion or the largest subspecies of tiger. This is due to the fact that the very large masses of Amur tigers known for the most part are considered insufficiently reliable . Science does not have sufficient data on the size and weight of representatives of the largest subspecies of lion (for example, the Barbary ). As for animals living in captivity, they are often a mixture of different subspecies. There is an opinion that lions in captivity are somewhat larger and heavier than tigers . The worried lion

The historical range of the lion was significantly wider than its modern range: as early as the Early Middle Ages, lions were found throughout Africa , except for deserts and tropical forests. They could also be seen in the Middle East , Iran , and even in some places in southern Europe (for example, they inhabited part of what is now southern Russia , rising to approximately the 45th parallel north). In northern and northwestern India, the lion was a common predator. However, persecution by humans and habitat destruction have led to the lion surviving in Africa only south of the Sahara; its range has now been greatly reduced. In Asia, a small lion population exists in the Gir Forest National Park (in the Indian state of Gujarat ), which, according to mtDNA, belongs to the subspecies Panthera leo leo (haplogroup B), and not to Panthera leo persica . Until the late Pleistocene , about 10,000 years ago, lions were the most widespread large mammals on Earth after humans . Their fossil remains have been found in much of Africa, across Eurasia from Western Europe to India , and in North America from the Yukon to Peru . The European cave lion , known from cave paintings , was apparently a subspecies of the modern lion. The lion's appearance is very distinctive. It is one of the few predators with pronounced sexual dimorphism . Males are not only significantly larger than females, but also have a mane, which in some subspecies reaches a large height and covers the shoulders, part of the back, and chest. Lions are yellowish-gray in color, with varying shades; the mane is often the same color as the pelt, but can be dark, even black. Lion subspecies are determined largely by the color of their mane. Aside from the mane, the animal's body fur is short, with only a tuft of long hair at the end of the tail. Lions primarily inhabit grassy savannas , but may occasionally migrate to arboreal savannas or dry tropical forests . Unlike other cats, they do not live alone, but in special family groups called prides . A pride typically consists of related females, offspring, and several adult males. Females hunt together, typically targeting large ungulates. Lions do not specifically hunt humans, but cannibalism is very common. Lions are apex predators , meaning they occupy a high position in the food chain and are preyed upon only by parasites . In the wild, lions live from ten to fourteen years; in captivity, they can live for over twenty years. Males, as a rule, rarely live more than ten years. This is because fights with other lions significantly reduce their lifespan.

Description Skeleton of a hunting lion. Oklahoma City Museum of Osteology. The lion holds the record for shoulder height among all felines. In terms of weight, the largest subspecies of lion and tiger are roughly comparable. Lions have powerful legs, strong jaws, and canines that are 8 cm long, so these predators are capable of killing fairly large animals. The lion's skull is very similar to that of a tiger; the frontal region is usually lowered and flatter. The nasal openings are wider than those of tigers. Nevertheless, the shape of the skull is very similar in these two species; there are differences only in the structure of the lower jaw.

Coloration varies from the color of buffalo skin to yellowish, reddish, or dark brown. The lower part of the lion's body is lighter than the upper part, the tip of the tail is black. Lion cubs are born with brown spots on their bodies, like those of leopards. At sexual maturity, these spots disappear, although in some adults, especially females, they remain on the abdomen and legs. Lions are one of the few land predators and the only members of the cat family to exhibit pronounced sexual dimorphism : lionesses are distinguished by their smaller size and lack of a mane. Females are more often hunters, and therefore lack a developed mane, which can interfere with camouflage. The color of the mane varies from white to black, and it always darkens with age. The average weight of adult lions is 150–250 kg for males and 120–182 kg for females. Nowell and Jackson calculated that the average weight of males is 181 kg, and that of females is 126 kg. A lion weighing 272 kg was shot in the mountains of Kenya. Sizes vary depending on the environment and habitat. Lions from Southern Africa are generally 5% heavier than those from the eastern part of the continent. The body length of a lion, including its head, reaches 170–250 cm for males and 140–175 cm for females. The shoulder height is about 123 cm for males and 107 cm for females. The tail length in lions ranges from 90 to 105 cm, and in lionesses from 70 to 100 cm. The maximum body length (3.3 m ) was possessed by a male with a black mane, killed in southern Angola in October 1973 . The heaviest known lion was a man-eater; it was shot in 1936 in Eastern Transvaal, South Africa. Its mass was 313 kg . Lions living in captivity, as a rule, weigh more than those predators that live in the wild. In 1970, a lion named Simba, weighing 375 kg, was registered at Colchester Zoo in the UK . As for the lion subspecies extinct in historical times, data on their size and weight are often insufficient and unreliable. However, it is possible that some of them, for example, the Barbary subspecies, significantly exceeded the largest modern lions in mass. The famous lion hunter Jules Gerard wrote in the mid-19th century that the carcass of an adult male weighed over 600 pounds (~272 kilograms). If this estimate is correct, then the Barbary lion, on average, exceeds in mass both modern lion subspecies and modern tiger subspecies. Furthermore, Gerard wrote about the size of male lion tracks being larger than the area of ​​a hand with fingers spread apart, about the difficulty of a human lifting the head of a killed lion from the ground, and about how the strongest man in their regiment collapsed when the skin and head of a killed lion were placed on him. A characteristic feature of both the lion and the lioness is a tuft of fur, a "brush," at the end of the tail; this tuft is approximately 5 cm long. At birth, the brush is absent, and begins to develop only from the cub's 5th month of life. By the age of 7 months, it is already clearly visible. Some individuals have a "spur" inside the brush, formed from the terminal sections of the tail vertebrae fused together.

Hunting and food A female lion opens her mouth Lions are powerful animals; they hunt in coordinated groups and pursue their chosen prey. However, they have less stamina than, for example, hyenas: in the latter, the heart accounts for 1% of their total body weight, while in lions it ranges from 0.45% (in males) to 0.57% (in females) . Therefore, lions can only run quickly over short distances [ 69 ] and must be close to their prey when attacking. They take advantage of factors that help them achieve this, such as hunting at night . Lions stalk a herd of ungulates until the distance is reduced to 30 meters or less. In most cases, several lionesses surround the herd from different sides. Once the herd is surrounded, they pounce on the nearest animal. Then comes a quick and powerful attack. Predators try to catch their prey with a quick leap. The animal usually dies from strangulation or suffocation. Small animals may be killed by a blow from the paw. The lion's main prey is large mammals such as wildebeest , zebra , buffalo , and warthogs in Africa, while in India, the lion hunts nilgai , wild boar , and several species of deer. Other animals hunted depend on their availability. These are primarily ungulates weighing between 50 and 300 kg, such as kudu , kongoni , oryx , and eland . Occasionally, lions may consume relatively small animals such as Thomson's gazelle or springbok . Because lions hunt in groups, they are capable of killing many large mammals, but in most cases they prefer to refrain from attacking adult giraffes to avoid injury. A lioness running at top speed in the Serengeti

General statistics collected during various studies have shown that lions feed on mammals weighing from 190 to 550 kg. In Africa, the main prey of lions are wildebeest and zebras. Adult elephants , hippopotamuses , rhinoceroses , impalas , and agile antelopes are excluded from the diet. However, giraffes and buffalo are quite often consumed by lions in some areas, for example, in the Kruger National Park . In the Manyara Park, due to the high population density, Cape buffalo make up 62% of the lions' diet. In rare cases, lions may also hunt hippopotamuses. Predators avoid adult rhinoceroses. Warthogs may also be eaten, despite their relatively small weight (about 180 kg). In some areas, prey includes animals that are not typical for lions' diet; this is the case in Chobe National Park , where they attack elephants . Park officials reported that the extremely hungry predators initially attacked cubs, then young animals, and then, at night, taking advantage of the elephants' poor eyesight, adult animals. Lions may also attack livestock; in India, this makes up a significant part of their diet [ 54 ] . They are also capable of killing other predators, such as servals , caracals , leopards , cheetahs , African wild dogs , and also eating animals that have died of disease or been killed by other predators . A lion can eat up to 30 kg of meat at one time [ 79 ] ; it rests for several hours before the next meal if the prey is not consumed in one sitting. An adult female requires on average about 5 kg of meat per day, and a male about 7 . Because lionesses hunt in open areas where their prey can easily spot them, coordinated actions lead to more successful hunts. This is especially true for larger mammals. Furthermore, a group of lionesses can protect their prey from other predators, such as hyenas, which are attracted by vultures flying over the carcass of a kill. Females do the bulk of the hunting. Males generally do not participate, except in cases where the prey is large, such as a giraffe or buffalo. Young individuals observe adult animals hunting, but begin to participate themselves at the age of one year. They are only capable of hunting fully at the age of two years.
